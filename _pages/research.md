As the Earth and the other planets orbit the Sun, the Solar System itself revolves around the center of the Milky Way. Far from being static and homogeneous, our Galaxy is a dynamic environment, continuously shaped by processes such as supernova events, stellar winds, and Galactic shear. Along its orbit, the Solar System has encountered a variety of environments with different gas densities and may at times have passed close to dying stars. At present, we are travelling through a giant cavity in the interstellar medium known as the Local Bubble.

My research aims to reconstruct and characterize the environments crossed by the Sun along its trajectory and investigate how they may have left detectable traces in Earth's geological records.

In previous work, I used radiative transfer simulations to generate synthetic skies and investigate how the Local Bubble influences polarization and Faraday rotation observations.
